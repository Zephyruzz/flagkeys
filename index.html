What it checks

Four ways text hides from you

Every trick leaves a trace in the file's XML. Flagkeys reads that XML directly, so nothing slips past just because it's invisible on screen.

Invisible font sizes

Text shrunk to 1pt or 2pt. Easy to scroll right past, but still part of the file.

w:sz < 12pt

White or near-white text

Words coloured to match the page. Hard to see on screen or on paper, but still in the file.

w:color ≈ #FFFFFF

Instruction phrases

Wording that reads like a command to an AI — “ignore previous instructions”, “include the word…”, “always respond with…”.

15 phrase patterns

Zero-width characters

Characters with no width at all, sitting between letters. Easy to miss, and they travel with the text when it's copied.

U+200B · U+200D · U+202E

Also checked: Word's “hidden text” setting, text-direction characters, and every header, footer, footnote, endnote and comment — including phrases split across several pieces of formatting.

Privacy

Your file never leaves your browser

There's no server that receives documents — nothing to upload to. The scan runs on your own device with code that's already on this page.

  • Read locally with JSZip — a .docx is a ZIP of XML files — and inspected in memory.
  • No uploads, no accounts, no analytics, no third-party scripts.
  • Works offline after your first visit. Close the tab and nothing remains.

Questions

Good to know

Does Flagkeys upload my file anywhere?

No. The file is opened by this page, inside your browser, and never sent over the network. You can turn off Wi-Fi after the page loads and the scan still works.

Does Flagkeys change my file?

No. It only reads the file and shows you what's inside. A flag also doesn't tell you why something is there — small print and invisible characters often have ordinary explanations.

The scan came back clean. Does that mean nothing is hidden?

A clean scan means none of the checks found anything in the document's text. It isn't a guarantee: text inside images, or text styled through Word's style sheet rather than directly, can slip through.

Why are some flags only “low risk”?

Some things look like instructions but are most likely meant for the reader — “Do not include pricing in the public version”, for example. Small 9–11pt text is common in footnotes too. Low-risk flags are worth a glance, not a worry.

Which file types does it support?

Only .docx today. More file types are planned, including PDF, spreadsheets, presentations and images. For a Google Doc, choose File → Download → Microsoft Word (.docx), then scan the downloaded file.
